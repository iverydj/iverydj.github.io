Research Interests

  • Study physics in solid-state materials: how defects & quasi-particles affect their electrical and optical properties.
  • Analyze defect states and local structure with transient spectroscopy (PICTS/DLTS) and tip-enhanced nanospectroscopy.
  • Develop and apply interpretable machine learning to extract physically meaningful information from experimental data.

Research

1. Defect Physics in Solid-State Materials

  • Defect states in oxide semiconductors and their relation to device characteristics and stability. (Published: Surf. Interfaces 2025; Sci. Rep. 2023; IEEE Trans. Electron Devices 2024; ACS Appl. Electron. Mater. 2024)
  • Defect-mediated control of carrier type in 2D semiconductors for complementary devices. (In preparation)
  • Exciton modulation in 2D semiconductors through coupling to plasmonic nanostructures. (Ongoing)

2. Defect and Nanoscale Spectroscopy

  • Transient spectroscopy (PICTS/DLTS) of defect states in 2D semiconductors. (Ongoing)
  • Nanoscale mapping of defects and strain in 2D materials by tip-enhanced spectroscopy. (Ongoing)
  • Electromagnetic simulation of near-field enhancement in tip-enhanced spectroscopy. (Published: Mater. Des. 2026)

3. Interpretable AI for Science

  • Interpretable machine learning relating Raman spectra to electrical properties. (In preparation)
  • Deep-learning noise reduction of experimental spectra that preserves spectral parameters. (In preparation)
  • Simulation of analog in-memory learning based on measured synaptic characteristics of ferroelectric InSe neuromorphic devices. (In preparation)
  • Numerical precision and the reliability of scientific machine learning. (Published: ICML 2026)
  • Review of the physical interpretability of machine-learning analysis in spectroscopy. (In preparation)
